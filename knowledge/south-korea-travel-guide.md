# South Korea Operations Brief

> Added in the Session 9 homework expansion. Revision 2026-08-01.

## Electronic travel authorisation

South Korea operates an electronic travel authorisation called K-ETA. It is not a
visa. It is a pre-clearance that visa-exempt travellers complete online before
boarding.

Sinaptik Travel's standing instruction is that the K-ETA requirement for any
given nationality must be re-checked on the official K-ETA portal before every
departure. The exemption list has changed several times and the operator does not
rely on a cached answer.

What Sinaptik Travel does control is its own timetable. K-ETA applications are
filed by the operator no later than fourteen calendar days before departure, and
the operator will not accept a South Korea booking inside twenty-one days.

An approved K-ETA is valid for multiple entries. Sinaptik Travel re-files it when
the traveller's passport changes, because the authorisation is bound to the
passport number rather than to the person.

## Arrival card

Travellers holding an approved K-ETA are exempt from the paper arrival card.
Travellers without one must complete it in flight. Sinaptik Travel's tour leaders
carry spare blank cards for this reason.

## Money and payments

The currency is the Korean won. Card acceptance is close to universal in Seoul
and Busan, and materially thinner in rural areas.

Sinaptik Travel issues every traveller a T-money transit card at the airport,
preloaded with KRW 20,000. T-money works on the Seoul metro, city buses,
intercity buses in most provinces, taxis, and convenience stores. It is refundable
at any convenience store minus a KRW 500 handling fee.

The operator does not issue cash advances. Travellers needing won on arrival are
directed to the airport bank counters, which the operator considers a better rate
than pre-departure exchange in Jakarta.

## Transport between cities

The KTX high-speed rail connects Seoul to Busan in roughly two and a half hours.
Sinaptik Travel books KTX seats at the time the itinerary is confirmed, never on
the day, because Friday and Sunday services sell out.

The Korea Rail Pass rarely pays for itself on itineraries with fewer than three
long-distance legs. The operator's rule of thumb is three legs or more, otherwise
point-to-point tickets.

## Seasons and what the operator refuses to schedule

Winter in Seoul is materially colder than winter in Tokyo. January lows sit well
below freezing and travellers routinely under-pack for it.

Spring blossom falls in early April and autumn colour in late October. Both
double accommodation prices, and Sinaptik Travel quotes a seasonal surcharge of
IDR 1,200,000 per traveller for departures inside those two windows.

The operator does not schedule outdoor hiking days between mid-July and
mid-August. That is monsoon season and the complaint rate on those itineraries
runs roughly three times the annual average.

## Connectivity

Sinaptik Travel includes a data eSIM on every South Korea package. The allowance
is 5 GB for itineraries up to seven days and 10 GB beyond that. Tethering is
permitted. The eSIM is activated on arrival, not before departure.

Public wifi is widespread and generally open, which is precisely why the operator
supplies the eSIM. Travellers are asked not to use banking apps on open networks.

## Health

Pharmacies are common but pharmacists outside Seoul may not speak English.
Sinaptik Travel prints a bilingual medication card for every traveller who
declares a prescription on the intake form.

Tap water is treated and drinkable. The operator still supplies bottled water on
transfer days because travellers ask for it.
