# Travel Insurance Concepts Brief

> Sinaptik Travel internal knowledge base. Course fixture for KelanaAI Session 9.
> Revision 2026-08-01. This brief explains the vocabulary. Product limits and
> premiums for the Sinaptik tiers live in `sinaptik-insurance-tiers.md`.

## Why the vocabulary matters

Most travel insurance disputes are not about whether something was covered. They
are about a word the traveller read one way and the insurer read another. Six
words carry almost all of that risk.

## Sum insured

The maximum the policy will pay for a benefit. It is a ceiling, not a payout. A
policy with a large medical sum insured still pays only what the hospital
actually billed.

Every benefit has its own sum insured. Medical, evacuation, baggage, and delay
are four separate ceilings, and exhausting one does not touch the others.

## Deductible

The portion the traveller pays before the insurer pays anything. A claim smaller
than the deductible produces no payment at all.

A lower premium almost always means a higher deductible. Comparing premiums
without comparing deductibles compares nothing.

## Sub-limit

A cap inside a cap. Baggage cover might have a sum insured of one figure, and a
per-article sub-limit of a much smaller one. A traveller who loses a single
expensive camera discovers the sub-limit, not the sum insured.

Electronics, jewellery, and cash carry sub-limits on almost every policy sold.

## Pre-existing condition

Any condition that existed, was treated, or showed symptoms before the policy
started. Insurers define the look-back window in days, and a condition inside
that window is excluded unless it was declared and accepted in writing.

Declaring a condition does not automatically exclude it. Not declaring it almost
always does.

## Medical evacuation versus repatriation

Medical evacuation moves a living patient to adequate care. Repatriation of
remains returns a body home. They are separate benefits with separate limits and
travellers routinely confuse them.

Evacuation is the single most expensive benefit in travel insurance. A charter
air ambulance across Asia runs into hundreds of millions of rupiah, which is why
evacuation limits differ so sharply between tiers.

## Prior authorisation

The requirement to call the assistance desk before incurring a cost. It exists so
the insurer can direct-bill the hospital instead of reimbursing later.

Skipping the call rarely voids a claim outright. It commonly reduces the payout
to the lowest tier's limit, and it always shifts the cash-flow burden onto the
traveller.

## Reimbursement versus direct billing

Under reimbursement the traveller pays and claims the money back afterwards.
Under direct billing the insurer settles with the provider and the traveller pays
nothing.

Direct billing is only available at network providers and only when the
assistance desk was called first. This is the practical reason the twenty-four
hour number is printed on the certificate.
