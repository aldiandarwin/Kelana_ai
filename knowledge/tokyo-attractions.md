## How Sinaptik Travel groups the city

Tokyo is too large to plan as one place. Sinaptik Travel splits every itinerary
into three zones and never books more than one zone per day. Travellers who
ignore this rule spend their holiday on trains.

The east zone covers Asakusa, Ueno, and Akihabara. It is the oldest part of the
itinerary and the cheapest to walk.

The centre zone covers the Imperial Palace grounds, Ginza, and Tsukiji Outer
Market. It suits a half day rather than a full one.

The west zone covers Shinjuku, Shibuya, Harajuku, and Meiji Jingu. It is the
most crowded and Sinaptik Travel schedules it on weekdays wherever possible.

## Sites that need booking ahead

Four sites cannot be walked into and are booked by Sinaptik Travel at the time
the itinerary is confirmed.

- teamLab digital art museums, timed entry, sells out roughly two weeks ahead
- Shibuya Sky observation deck, timed entry, sunset slots sell out first
- Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, lottery allocation, applications open monthly
- Tokyo Skytree Tembo Galleria, timed entry, cheaper if booked before arrival

Sinaptik Travel charges no booking fee on these four. The operator absorbs the
cost because a failed booking is the single most common complaint on Japan
itineraries.

## Free sites worth a slot

Meiji Jingu, the Imperial Palace East Gardens, Yoyogi Park, and the Tokyo
Metropolitan Government Building observation decks all cost nothing. The
government building decks are the operator's standard substitute when Shibuya
Sky is sold out.

Senso-ji temple in Asakusa is free to enter. The approach street, Nakamise, is
busiest between ten in the morning and four in the afternoon.

## Getting around

A prepaid IC card, Suica or Pasmo, works on almost every train and bus and on
many vending machines and convenience stores. Sinaptik Travel issues one to
every traveller at the airport and loads it with JPY 3,000.

The Japan Rail Pass rarely pays for itself on a Tokyo-only itinerary. Sinaptik
Travel recommends it only when the trip includes at least one long-distance
return leg, for example Tokyo to Kyoto and back.

## Timing notes

Cherry blossom season falls in late March and early April and doubles hotel
prices. Autumn colour falls in late November. The rainy season runs through June.

Sinaptik Travel does not schedule outdoor-heavy days in August. Heat and humidity
in Tokyo in August produce more itinerary complaints than any other month.
